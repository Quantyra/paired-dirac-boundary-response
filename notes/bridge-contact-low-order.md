# Lower-order contact coefficients from the bridge split

Status: ordinary periodic-model calculation, not Lean-certified and not a
proof of the physical paired bridge or its regulator limit. It compares the
zeroth and first powers of t from the split used in
[the BV route](bridge-zeroth-bv-route.md) and
[the quadratic term](bridge-quadratic-bv.md) with Eq. (5) of the pinned
[variable-contact source](https://github.com/Quantyra/quantyra-wormhole-semiclassical/blob/f0eab59063422c80ccc7c8e281170c75cc3afc71/notes/current-variable-contact-conversion.md).

## Setup and result

Assume smooth real periodic f,u,v with f>0 and the source's bridge
normalization 1/(2π). Write t=h² and retain the zeroth exponential term
and the term z_h=h²B_h²/(4A_h). The leading and t coefficients of their
sum are

    (1/(4π)) ∫ (dy/f) { 2uv
                  +t[−(R/6)uv−(1/3)u'v'] },             (1)
    R=2f''/f−4(f'/f)².

Here dy/f is the source's time-reduced measure and the normal gradient
pairing in this proper-distance coordinate is u'v'. Formula (1) matches
the corresponding terms in the source contact expansion under those
measure and bridge conventions. The t² term and physical paired geometry
are not established by this comparison.

## Derivation

Let V_h=∫v(y+hξ_s)ds and p=f². For the zeroth exponential term put
H(V,A)=V A^(−1/2), α=(v',2ff'), β=(v'',2[(f')²+ff'']). The bridge
moments E∫ξ_s²ds=1/3 and E(∫ξ_sds)²=1/6 give

    J''(0)=∫u [ (1/3)∇H·β+(1/6)αᵀ Hess(H)α ]dy,

where H derivatives are evaluated at (v,f²). Direct differentiation yields

    J''(0)=∫u [ v''/(3f) −v'f'/(3f²)
                 −v f''/(3f²)+v(f')²/(6f³) ]dy.          (2)

The zeroth exponential term contributes J(0)/(2π) at order zero and
h²J''(0)/(4π) at order h². The z_h term contributes at order h²

    h²/(8π) ∫u v(f')²/f³ dy.

Combining it with (2), the coefficient inside the 1/(4π) integral is

    (1/3)(v'/f)' − (v/3) f''/f² +(2v/3)(f')²/f³.

Periodic integration by parts changes the first term paired with u to
−u'v'/(3f); the remaining two terms equal −uv R/(6f). The order-zero
term is (1/(2π))∫uv/f, giving (1). This uses no frequency truncation.

For the BV setting, smooth periodic approximants with a common positive
lower bound can pass this lower-order identity using convergence of f,f',
f'',u,v and their needed derivatives in the stated norms. A physical
half-domain version must retain or cancel the boundary term generated by
integration by parts; the periodic proof alone cannot discard it. Matching
the source's t² coefficient, thermal images, angular regulator and actual
bundle remains an independent task. The calculation does not establish a
uniform curved mixed-response remainder.

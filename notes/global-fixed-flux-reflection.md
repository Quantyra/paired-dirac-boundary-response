# Global fixed-flux reflection on a smooth disk-sphere product

Status: ordinary geometric construction for the specified static product
model. It supplies a global lift under explicit smooth warped-product and
matched-spin hypotheses; it does not identify the physical copy map J or
certify this argument in Lean. It complements
[the closed-domain lemma](reflection-closed-domain-covariance.md) and the
[pinned reflection source](https://github.com/Quantyra/quantyra-wormhole-semiclassical/blob/f0eab59063422c80ccc7c8e281170c75cc3afc71/notes/recentered-stationary-constraints.md).

## Construction and hypotheses

Let X=D²×S² with a smooth rotationally invariant warped metric

g=g_D+a² g_S,

where g_D is a smooth rotationally invariant disk metric, a is a positive
smooth rotationally invariant function on the disk, and g_S is the round
sphere metric. The disk includes its center and the outer boundary is a
rotation orbit. Take the product spin structure, a Hermitian monopole line
bundle L pulled back from S², and its unitary connection pulled back from
S². Let r reflect the disk angle θ to -θ and fix the sphere. Then r is a
smooth isometric involution, including at the center, and preserves the
outer boundary and the magnetic connection.

The spinor module of the product can be represented as the graded tensor
product of the disk and sphere spin modules. In an oriented radial disk
frame, take the source's paired Clifford matrices and define

    U = 1_copy ⊗ σ₁ ⊗ c_S,       (Rψ)(x)=U_x ψ(r x).

Here c_S is sphere chirality and the displayed fiber map is read between
the corresponding reflected radial frames. The copy factor is a trivial
rank-two bundle in this statement. On the punctured disk the two frames at
x and r x have the same radial normal and opposite angular direction under
dr, so the local identities UγθU=-γθ, UγnU=γn, UγAU=γA implement dr.

## Patching and center

On overlapping oriented sphere spin frames, c_S commutes with the spin
transition because it is the oriented two-dimensional Clifford volume.
It also commutes with the scalar U(1) transition of L. The disk factor is
independent of sphere coordinates, so the local maps agree on sphere and
monopole overlaps, including the north/south gauge overlap. There is no
complex conjugation of L and no reversal of the sphere.

To check the disk center, choose a smooth Cartesian spin frame on the disk.
Let S(θ) be the spin change from this frame to the radial frame on a
punctured neighborhood. The generator of S is the disk Clifford bivector;
U anticommutes with that generator. Consequently U S(-θ)=S(θ) U, with the
consistent convention for the frame change, and the reflected radial
expression has a constant Cartesian representative. This representative
extends smoothly to the center. The sign acquired by S under θ->θ+2π occurs
on both sides of the expression and cancels. Away from the center radial
frames patch by the same identity. Thus R is a globally defined smooth
fiberwise unitary involution; R²=1 follows by applying the reflection twice
and U²=1. The construction uses the full Dirac module and is a reflection
lift, not an orientation-preserving Spin(4) frame transformation.

## Connection, operator and domain

In a local orthonormal frame, the spin connection is built from the
Levi-Civita connection one-forms and the Clifford bivectors. Since r is an
isometry, the pullback Levi-Civita connection is the connection of the
reflected frame. The above lift implements that frame reflection on all
Clifford generators, so conjugation carries each bivector and hence its
spin-connection term to the corresponding reflected term. This includes
the mixed disk-sphere connection terms generated by the warp factor a.
The pulled-back magnetic connection is unchanged, and its scalar action
commutes with U. Therefore R intertwines the twisted spin connection and
commutes with the associated Dirac differential expression. Any additional
real scalar mass depending only on the disk radius commutes with R as well.

If the paired boundary involution is χ=τ₃γn in these same boundary frames,
then [U,χ]=0, and r preserves the outward normal. The induced boundary map
therefore intertwines χ. With the H¹ projector realization and closedness
assumed in the domain lemma, R preserves both complementary first-order
domains, their adjoints, and the composed square domains, and commutes with
the corresponding resolvents and heat semigroups. No extra condition is
imposed at the smooth center.

## Scope of the application

The argument uses a smooth rotationally invariant finite-cut metric, a
sphere-pulled-back magnetic connection and the indicated product and copy
identifications. A nonzero time component of an orbit gauge field is odd
under r: reflection then relates the operator at Aθ to the operator at
-Aθ, and is a symmetry only when the relevant field is invariant. The
physical source must still be checked against the stated product-spin and
copy conventions and the H¹ closed-domain hypotheses. The reflection R
preserves χ; it is not the copy-exchanging partner J, which reverses χ.
Thus this construction does not prove the source-to-J identification, the
full tangent response, a regulator limit, or a curved trace theorem. No
numerical computation is needed for this geometric argument.

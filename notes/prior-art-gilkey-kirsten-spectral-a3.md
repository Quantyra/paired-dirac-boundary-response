# Prior art: Gilkey--Kirsten spectral boundary coefficient

This compares the primary preprint [Heat asymptotics with spectral boundary conditions II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0007015), arXiv:math-ph/0007015v1, with the current paper-one draft. It is a focused comparison, not a novelty verdict or a proof of the physical response.

In sections 1--2, Gilkey and Kirsten consider a Dirac-type elliptic complex on a compact smooth m-dimensional manifold with smooth boundary and the associated positive square P_B*P_B. Their boundary projection is onto the nonnegative spectrum of a self-adjoint tangential Dirac-type operator A constructed from frozen boundary data and an auxiliary endomorphism; this is a spectral, generally nonlocal boundary condition. With a smooth smearing endomorphism F, they state the small-time heat-trace expansion and calculate the local a3 coefficient in Theorem 1 for m>=4, including boundary curvature and normal smearing derivatives. Their introduction notes that the full expansion can contain logarithmic terms, but the theorem highlighted here is the a3 coefficient, not the candidate's logarithmic mixed-response coefficient.

The manuscript's propositions 4.1--4.2 instead concern a declared two-dimensional flat tangent mixed second metric response with a local complementary projector, specific even-reflection and regularity assumptions, and an explicitly subtracted Fourier multiplier. In particular the draft's t^(5/2) term and conditional t^3 log t term are coefficients of that differentiated and subtracted response, not directly the a3 invariant of the cited m>=4 spectral-boundary problem. The manuscript's separate four-dimensional physical problem and its mode decomposition are not proved equivalent to that flat diagnostic.

This source shows that Dirac square heat asymptotics, spectral projectors, boundary invariants and normal smearing derivatives have substantial prior theory. A different boundary projector, dimension or observable does not alone prove novelty. The concrete comparison still needs the actual paired square boundary jets, physical normalization, singular profile and complete mixed variation; logarithmic spectral-boundary prior art of Gilkey--Grubb is analyzed separately in `prior-art-spectral-boundary-logarithms.md`. No assertion is made that Theorem 1 proves or excludes the draft's coefficient, nor that the prior-art search is complete.
